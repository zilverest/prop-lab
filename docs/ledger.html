How it works

Every few hours, the system checks player-prop lines across ~15 sportsbooks and exchanges (DraftKings, FanDuel, Hard Rock, Kalshi, Polymarket, and others). For each prop it strips out the house's cut to get a no-vig "fair" probability, then averages that across every book pricing it — that's the market consensus.

A prop only becomes a candidate if it clears three checks: at least 3 books are pricing the exact same line, the fair probability sits in a normal 30–70% range (extreme longshot lines produce math artifacts, not real edges, so they're excluded entirely), and the gap between one book's price and the consensus is at least 3% — big enough to not be rounding noise.

One cleared prop can become more than one slip below — a straight bet, and it may also feed a cross-game parlay or a same-game parlay — so the slip counts above are not a count of distinct edges found. The clearance rate is the number that actually says how rare a real disagreement is. A low, stable rate over the season is the expected, honest outcome — it would mean these markets are efficient, which is more likely true than not.

Passing these checks doesn't prove a bet is right — it just means it's worth tracking. The real test is closing line value: does the market move toward our number before kickoff? That's measured separately and isn't shown on this page, but it's what ultimately decides whether any of this is signal.
